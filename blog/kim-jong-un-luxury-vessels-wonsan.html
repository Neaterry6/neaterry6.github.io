Satellite images show Kim Jong Un's summer fleet, including an 80m yacht with a 50m pool

BBC News · 2026-10-18 · 0 views

An 80m (263ft) luxury vessel has been repeatedly spotted in satellite images off North Korea's Wonsan coast from July to mid-October this year. What stands out is the 50m swimming pool along the deck, with a cluster of waterslides at one end.

After sitting idle for months at a maintenance facility, it moved in early July to waters off the Kalma Peninsula, close to Wonsan — a seaside resort with a sprawling villa used by Kim Jong Un and his family.

The BBC tracked four large vessels around Wonsan, measuring roughly 50m, 55m, 60m and 80m. All four appeared repeatedly around Kim's villa this year. The 50m and 55m boats were seen until the end of August near Tae and Sin islands; the 60m one was near the villa as recently as last week. Satellite imagery also showed four smaller boats alongside the 50m vessel — an expert quoted by the BBC says they ferried people, fuel and supplies to it.

Historical imagery shows a large vessel with the same distinctive characteristics in Wonsan in 2002, when Kim Jong Il ruled. In 2013, Dennis Rodman described a 60m boat he used during a visit as a cross between a ferry and a Disney boat.

The numbers behind it

Nobody outside the regime knows what these boats cost. Comparable luxury yachts run from tens of millions to over $100m. Kim's much smaller 29-metre Princess yacht was previously estimated at about $7m.

Against that: the UN classifies North Korea as one of the world's poorest countries, and a 2025 UN report says most North Koreans live under "systematic, widespread and gross human rights violations", with chronic food shortages leaving an estimated 40% of the population undernourished. Per capita GDP was around $667 in 2024, against South Korea's $36,227.

Why it matters

  • This is what satellite imagery is actually good for. Individual boat locations cannot prove where a person is, but the pattern — vessels moving out in summer, overlapping with gaps in Kim's public appearances — is evidence of a life the state never shows.
  • State media frames Kim's trips as official inspections and guidance. Defectors told the BBC the family holidays in Wonsan were an open secret at least among some North Koreans, which makes the propaganda a matter of presentation rather than secrecy.
  • The contrast is the point. Workers, office employees and cooperative farmers are legally entitled to 14 days of annual leave, defectors say, and it is rarely honoured. Travel outside your city or county needs official permission. A holiday is not just expensive there, it is a privilege of rank.
  • Even the new resort is priced for someone else. Kalma opened in July 2025 and state media says it can host nearly 20,000 visitors; a South Korean report, citing an unnamed source, says locals were offered four-night packages for around $100 — a substantial sum where GDP per capita is $667.

Ri Jong Ho, a former official in the Workers' Party's Office 39, put it plainly: Kim "has four luxury vessels like these while ordinary people are struggling just to get one meal a day".
