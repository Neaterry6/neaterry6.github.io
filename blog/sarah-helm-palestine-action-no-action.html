Police dropped the case against the national security adviser's wife over one sentence on X

BBC News · 2026-10-18 · 0 views

The Metropolitan Police will take no further action against Sarah Helm, the wife of the government's national security adviser Jonathan Powell, over a post she made on X referencing Palestine Action.

Helm wrote on Thursday: "God help us authors who support Palestine and Palestine Action." She was responding to a discussion about the author Sally Rooney.

On Friday the Met said it was "in the process of being assessed to determine whether any further police investigation may be required" after being made aware of the post. By Saturday it had concluded: specialist officers assessed it, "it was determined that it did not meet the criminal threshold", and "no further police action will be taken".

How the post fits into a bigger row

Palestine Action was proscribed as a terrorist organisation in July 2025. Supporting the group is a criminal offence punishable by up to 14 years in prison.

Rooney, the bestselling author of Normal People and Conversations with Friends, said she would donate earnings from her books to the group, which states its goal as "ending global participation in Israel's genocidal and apartheid regime". Her publisher Faber then determined it could not sell her novels in the UK.

Why it matters

  • The timeline is the uncomfortable part. A single sentence, posted Thursday, was under police assessment by Friday and cleared by Saturday. Whatever the merits, that is a national police force spending specialist-officer time on a tweet by a journalist married to a senior official, in a case that had already been picked up nationally.
  • "Did not meet the criminal threshold" is a narrow finding, not a vindication of the law. It says this post fell short of the offence, not that the offence is drawn correctly. The proscription carries up to 14 years, and the line between support and comment is being worked out case by case, by police, in public.
  • The asymmetry is what people will notice. Faber concluded it could not sell Rooney's novels in the UK over her stated intent to fund the group, while a post from a much more prominent household is closed with no action in 48 hours. Both outcomes may be legally right; they do not feel like the same system.
  • This is a free-expression question being decided by discretion rather than by statute. The Met chose not to pursue; the next force, or the next post, may choose differently, and there is no published bright line for anyone to rely on.
